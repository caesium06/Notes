# 4. Fast Mental Calculation Tricks

## Trick 1: Calculate 10%, 5%, 1% and 0.1%

These are the building blocks of percentage calculations.

For 10%: Divide by 10.

For 5%: Divide by 20.

For 1%: Divide by 100.

For 0.1%: Divide by 1000.

Example: Find 15% of 840.

- 10% = 84
- 5% = 42
- 15% = 84 + 42 = **126**

Example: Find 21% of 600.

- 20% = 120
- 1% = 6
- 21% = **126**

## Trick 2: Calculate 25% and 50%

- 50% = Divide by 2.
- 25% = Divide by 4.
- 75% = 3/4.
- 12.5% = Divide by 8.

Example: Find 75% of 640.

640 × 3/4 = 480.

Example: Find 12.5% of 800.

800 ÷ 8 = **100**.

## Trick 3: Interchange Percentage and Number

**a% of b = b% of a**

This is one of the most useful SSC shortcuts.

Example:

16% of 25 = 25% of 16

= 16 ÷ 4

= **4**

Another example:

32% of 25 = 25% of 32

= 32 ÷ 4

= **8**

Example:

48% of 50 = 50% of 48

= 48 ÷ 2

= **24**

## Trick 4: Calculate 99%, 98% and 95%

Instead of multiplying directly, subtract the missing percentage.

Example: Find 99% of 800.

= 800 − 1% of 800

= 800 − 8

= **792**

Example: Find 95% of 600.

= 600 − 5% of 600

= 600 − 30

= **570**

## Trick 5: Calculate 125%, 150% and 200%

| Percentage | Shortcut |
|---|---|
| 125% | Multiply by 5/4 |
| 150% | Multiply by 3/2 |
| 200% | Multiply by 2 |
| 250% | Multiply by 5/2 |
| 300% | Multiply by 3 |

Example: 125% of 240

= 240 × 5/4

= **300**

Example: 150% of 400

= 400 × 3/2

= **600**

## Trick 6: Break Complicated Percentages

Example: Find 35% of 480.

= 30% + 5%

= 144 + 24

= **168**

Example: Find 17% of 600.

= 10% + 5% + 2%

= 60 + 30 + 12

= **102**

Example: Find 42% of 250.

= 40% + 2%

= 100 + 5

= **105**

# 5. Percentage Increase and Decrease

### Percentage Increase

Formula:

Percentage increase = (Increase / Original value) × 100

Example:

A product price increases from ₹400 to ₹500.

Increase = 500 − 400 = 100

Percentage increase = (100/400) × 100

= **25%**

### Percentage Decrease

Formula:

Percentage decrease = (Decrease / Original value) × 100

Example:

A product price decreases from ₹800 to ₹600.

Decrease = 200

Percentage decrease = (200/800) × 100

= **25%**

### Important: Always use the original value as the denominator.

---

# 6. Successive Percentage Change

When two percentage changes occur successively, use:

Net change = a + b + (ab/100)

Here:
- Increase = Positive value.
- Decrease = Negative value.

### Case 1: Two successive increases

A value increases by 20% and then 10%.

Net change:

= 20 + 10 + (20 × 10)/100

= 30 + 2

= **32% increase**

### Case 2: Two successive decreases

A value decreases by 20% and then 10%.

= -20 - 10 + (-20 × -10)/100

= -30 + 2

= **28% decrease**

### Case 3: Increase followed by decrease

A value increases by 20% and then decreases by 20%.

= 20 - 20 + (20 × -20)/100

= -4%

**Result: 4% decrease.**

Important: Equal percentage increase and decrease do not cancel each other.

# 7. Important Percentage Relationships

### Relationship 1: A is x% more than B

If B = 100, then A = 100 + x.

Example:

A is 25% more than B.

If B = 100, A = 125.

Therefore, B is less than A by:

(25/125) × 100 = **20%**

### Relationship 2: A is x% less than B

If B = 100, then A = 100 − x.

Example:

A is 20% less than B.

If B = 100, A = 80.

Therefore, B is more than A by:

(20/80) × 100 = **25%**

### Important conversion table

| A is more than B by | B is less than A by |
|---|---:|
| 10% | 9.09% |
| 20% | 16.67% |
| 25% | 20% |
| 30% | 23.08% |
| 40% | 28.57% |
| 50% | 33.33% |
| 60% | 37.5% |
| 75% | 42.86% |
| 100% | 50% |

Formula:

If A is x% more than B, then B is less than A by:

[x / (100 + x)] × 100

If A is x% less than B, then B is more than A by:

[x / (100 − x)] × 100

# 8. Population and Depreciation

### Population Increase

If population increases by r% annually:

Final population = Initial population × (1 + r/100)^n

Example:

Population = 10,000

Annual increase = 10%

Time = 2 years

Final population:

= 10,000 × (1.1)²

= 10,000 × 1.21

= **12,100**

### Depreciation

If value decreases by r% annually:

Final value = Initial value × (1 − r/100)^n

Example:

A machine costs ₹50,000 and depreciates by 10% annually for two years.

Final value:

= 50,000 × (0.9)²

= 50,000 × 0.81

= **₹40,500**

# 9. Percentage Change in Consumption and Expenditure

When price increases, how much should consumption decrease to keep expenditure unchanged?

Formula:

Required decrease in consumption = [Increase / (100 + Increase)] × 100

Example:

Price increases by 25%.

Required decrease in consumption:

= (25/125) × 100

= **20%**

When price decreases, how much can consumption increase without changing expenditure?

Formula:

Permissible increase in consumption = [Decrease / (100 − Decrease)] × 100

Example:

Price decreases by 20%.

Permissible increase:

= (20/80) × 100

= **25%**

# 10. Important Things to Remember for Fast Calculations

1. Memorise fraction-to-percentage conversions from 1/2 to 1/16.
2. Remember 10%, 20%, 25%, 33.33%, 50%, 75% and 12.5%.
3. Use a% of b = b% of a whenever it makes calculation easier.
4. Calculate 5% by dividing by 20.
5. Calculate 25% by dividing by 4.
6. Calculate 12.5% by dividing by 8.
7. Calculate 75% by multiplying by 3 and dividing by 4.
8. Calculate 99% by subtracting 1%.
9. For percentage increase/decrease, always use the original value.
10. Equal successive increases and decreases do not cancel each other.
11. A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease gives a 4% net decrease.
12. Convert percentages into fractions before multiplying whenever possible.
13. Cancel common factors before multiplication to avoid large numbers.
14. Do not confuse percentage points with percentage change.
15. In successive percentage questions, use the net change formula.

**Five formulas to remember:**

1. Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100
2. Percentage change = (Change / Original value) × 100
3. Successive change = a + b + ab/100
4. Required consumption decrease = Increase / (100 + Increase) × 100
5. Required consumption increase = Decrease / (100 − Decrease) × 100

**Golden shortcut:** Whenever you see a percentage calculation, first check whether you can convert it into a simple fraction or interchange the percentage and number. This often saves more time than direct multiplication.
