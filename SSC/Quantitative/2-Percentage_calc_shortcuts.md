## 3. Quick Percentage Calculation Tricks

### Trick 1: Find 10%, 1%, and 5%

- 10%: Divide by 10.
- 1%: Divide by 100.
- 5%: Find 10% and divide by 2.

Example: Find 15% of 800.

\[
10\%=80
\]

\[
5\%=40
\]

\[
15\%=80+40=\boxed{120}
\]

### Trick 2: Reverse the Calculation

Instead of calculating a difficult percentage directly, reverse the multiplication.

Example: Find 25% of 640.

\[
25\%=\frac{1}{4}
\]

\[
640\times\frac{1}{4}=\boxed{160}
\]

### Trick 3: Interchange the Numbers

\[
x\%\text{ of }y=y\%\text{ of }x
\]

Example:

\[
16\%\text{ of }25=25\%\text{ of }16
\]

\[
=4
\]

This is useful when one of the numbers has a convenient percentage.

### Trick 4: Calculate 99%, 98%, and 95%

Instead of direct multiplication, subtract the missing percentage.

Example: Find 95% of 600.

\[
100\%-5\%
\]

\[
600-30=\boxed{570}
\]

## 4. Percentage Increase and Decrease

### Percentage Increase

\[
\text{New Value}=\text{Original Value}\times
\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)
\]

Example: A price of ₹500 increases by 20%.

\[
500\times\frac{120}{100}=\boxed{600}
\]

### Percentage Decrease

\[
\text{New Value}=\text{Original Value}\times
\left(1-\frac{R}{100}\right)
\]

Example: A price of ₹800 decreases by 25%.

\[
800\times\frac{75}{100}=\boxed{600}
\]

## 5. Successive Percentage Changes

When two percentage changes occur successively:

\[
\text{Net Change}=a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
\]

Use a negative sign for a decrease.

Example: A value increases by 20% and then decreases by 10%.

\[
20-10+\frac{20\times(-10)}{100}
\]

\[
=10-2=\boxed{8\%\text{ increase}}
\]

### Important shortcuts

| Changes | Net result |
|---|---:|
| +10%, +10% | 21% increase |
| +20%, +20% | 44% increase |
| -10%, -10% | 19% decrease |
| +10%, -10% | 1% decrease |
| +20%, -20% | 4% decrease |

## 6. Comparison of Percentages

### A is what percentage of B?

\[
\frac{A}{B}\times100
\]

Example: 40 is what percentage of 200?

\[
\frac{40}{200}\times100=20\%
\]

### A is x% more than B

\[
\frac{A-B}{B}\times100
\]

### A is x% less than B

\[
\frac{B-A}{B}\times100
\]

**Important:** Always use the original comparison value as the denominator.

## 7. Population and Depreciation

### Population Increase

\[
P_n=P\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)^n
\]

### Population Decrease

\[
P_n=P\left(1-\frac{R}{100}\right)^n
\]

Where:

- \(P\) = Initial population
- \(R\) = Annual percentage change
- \(n\) = Number of years

Example: Population of a town is 10,000. It increases by 10% annually for 2 years.

\[
P_2=10000\times\left(\frac{110}{100}\right)^2
\]

\[
=10000\times1.21=\boxed{12100}
\]

## 8. Expenditure and Consumption

If the price of a commodity increases by \(x\%\), the reduction in consumption required to keep expenditure unchanged is:

\[
\frac{x}{100+x}\times100
\]

Example: Price increases by 25%.

\[
\frac{25}{125}\times100=\boxed{20\%}
\]

If price decreases by \(x\%\), the increase in consumption possible without changing expenditure is:

\[
\frac{x}{100-x}\times100
\]

Example: Price decreases by 20%.

\[
\frac{20}{80}\times100=\boxed{25\%}
\]

## 11. Important Exam Reminders

1. Memorise common fraction-to-percentage conversions.
2. Use fraction multiplication instead of long percentage calculations whenever possible.
3. In successive percentage changes, remember that the second change applies to the changed value.
4. For expenditure questions, distinguish between price change and consumption change.
5. In comparison questions, carefully identify the reference value.
6. Practise mental calculations to improve speed in SSC CGL Quantitative Aptitude.
