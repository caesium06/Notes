# English Grammar – Present Tense
## SSC CGL Preparation Notes

Present tense describes actions happening now, habitual actions, general truths, and actions that started in the past and continue into the present.

There are **four types of present tense**:

1. Simple Present Tense
2. Present Continuous Tense
3. Present Perfect Tense
4. Present Perfect Continuous Tense

---

# 1. Simple Present Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + V1 (s/es) + Object |
| Negative | Subject + do/does + not + V1 |
| Interrogative | Do/Does + Subject + V1? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Singular third-person subjects take s/es.**

- He plays cricket.
- She goes to school.
- Ram watches television.

**Rule 2: Plural subjects and I/You take V1.**

- They play cricket.
- I work every day.
- You speak English.

**Rule 3: Use Simple Present for habitual actions.**

- He wakes up early.
- She goes to the gym daily.

**Rule 4: Use Simple Present for universal truths and scientific facts.**

- The sun rises in the east.
- Water boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.

**Rule 5: Use Simple Present for fixed schedules and timetables.**

- The train leaves at 6 AM.
- The examination starts at 10 AM.

**Rule 6: Do/Does is followed by V1.**

Incorrect: He does not goes there.

Correct: He does not go there.

Incorrect: Does she likes coffee?

Correct: Does she like coffee?

### Important subject-verb agreement

| Subject | Verb |
|---|---|
| He / She / It | Works |
| Ram / John | Works |
| I / We / You / They | Work |
| The boys | Work |
| Each student | Works |
| Everyone | Works |

---

# 2. Present Continuous Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + am/is/are + V-ing |
| Negative | Subject + am/is/are + not + V-ing |
| Interrogative | Am/Is/Are + Subject + V-ing? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Actions happening at the moment of speaking.**

- I am studying English.
- She is cooking food.
- They are playing football.

**Rule 2: Temporary actions or situations.**

- He is staying in Kochi this week.
- I am working on a new project.

**Rule 3: Changing or developing situations.**

- The population is increasing.
- Technology is developing rapidly.

**Rule 4: Planned future arrangements.**

- I am meeting my friend tomorrow.
- She is travelling next week.

### Helping verb selection

| Subject | Helping verb |
|---|---|
| I | Am |
| He / She / It | Is |
| Singular noun | Is |
| We / You / They | Are |
| Plural noun | Are |

### Important: Stative verbs

Some verbs generally do not take the continuous form when expressing a state.

Examples:

- Know
- Believe
- Understand
- Love
- Hate
- Own
- Belong
- Prefer
- Need
- Want
- Remember
- Seem

Incorrect: I am knowing the answer.

Correct: I know the answer.

Incorrect: She is owning a car.

Correct: She owns a car.

Note: Some verbs can be used in continuous forms when their meanings change.

Example:
- I think he is correct. (Opinion)
- I am thinking about the problem. (Considering)

# 3. Present Perfect Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + has/have + V3 |
| Negative | Subject + has/have + not + V3 |
| Interrogative | Has/Have + Subject + V3? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Recently completed actions.**

- I have completed my homework.
- She has just arrived.

**Rule 2: Past actions with present results.**

- I have lost my keys. (I do not have them now.)
- He has broken his leg.

**Rule 3: Life experiences without specifying an exact past time.**

- I have visited Delhi.
- She has never travelled abroad.

**Rule 4: Use since and for with situations continuing up to the present.**

- I have lived here for five years.
- He has worked here since 2020.

### Has vs Have

| Subject | Helping verb |
|---|---|
| He / She / It | Has |
| Singular noun | Has |
| I / We / You / They | Have |
| Plural noun | Have |

### Important SSC rules

**1. Since vs For**

- Since = Starting point of time.
- For = Duration of time.

Examples:

- Since Monday
- Since 2020
- Since morning
- For two hours
- For five years
- For a long time

**2. Present Perfect with definite past time**

Do not normally use Present Perfect with a definite finished past-time expression.

Incorrect: I have met him yesterday.

Correct: I met him yesterday.

Incorrect: She has completed the work last week.

Correct: She completed the work last week.

Common past-time indicators:
- Yesterday
- Last night
- Last year
- Two days ago
- In 2010

**3. This is the first time**

Use Present Perfect.

- This is the first time I have visited Kerala.
- This is the first time she has driven a car.

---

# 4. Present Perfect Continuous Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + has/have + been + V-ing |
| Negative | Subject + has/have + not + been + V-ing |
| Interrogative | Has/Have + Subject + been + V-ing? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Actions that started in the past and are still continuing.**

- I have been studying for three hours.
- She has been working here since 2022.

**Rule 2: Actions that have recently stopped but have visible present results.**

- He is tired because he has been running.
- The ground is wet because it has been raining.

### Since and For

- I have been waiting since 8 AM.
- I have been waiting for two hours.

### Has been vs Have been

| Subject | Structure |
|---|---|
| He / She / It | Has been + V-ing |
| I / We / You / They | Have been + V-ing |

---

# 5. Comparison of All Four Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | V1 / V1+s/es | He works daily. |
| Present Continuous | Am/is/are + V-ing | He is working now. |
| Present Perfect | Has/have + V3 | He has worked. |
| Present Perfect Continuous | Has/have been + V-ing | He has been working for two hours. |

### Important difference

Consider the verb "study":

1. I study English. – Habitual action.
2. I am studying English. – Happening now.
3. I have studied English. – Completed action with present relevance or experience.
4. I have been studying English for two hours. – Started in the past and continues now.

# 6. Important SSC Exam Rules and Traps

### Rule 1: Each, Every, Everyone, Somebody, Nobody

These generally take singular verbs.

- Everyone is present.
- Each student has a book.
- Nobody knows the answer.

### Rule 2: Either / Neither

When used independently as subjects, they generally take singular verbs.

- Neither of the boys is present.
- Either of the answers is correct.

### Rule 3: One of + Plural Noun

The verb is singular.

- One of my friends is a doctor.
- One of the students has won the prize.

### Rule 4: A number of vs The number of

- A number of students are absent.
- The number of students is increasing.

### Rule 5: Along with / As well as / Together with

The verb agrees with the main subject.

- Ram, along with his friends, is going.
- The teacher, as well as the students, is present.

### Rule 6: News, Mathematics, Physics

These generally take singular verbs.

- The news is shocking.
- Mathematics is difficult.

### Rule 7: Uncountable nouns

Generally take singular verbs.

- The information is useful.
- The furniture is expensive.
- The advice is valuable.

### Rule 8: Conditional sentences

In first conditional sentences:

If + Simple Present, Subject + will + V1.

- If it rains, I will stay home.
- If he studies, he will pass.

Incorrect: If he will study, he will pass.

Correct: If he studies, he will pass.

### Rule 9: Time clauses

After when, before, after, until, as soon as, etc., use Simple Present to refer to the future in a time clause.

- I will call you when I reach home.
- Wait until he arrives.

### Rule 10: It is time / It is high time

Use Simple Past after these expressions when referring to an action that should happen now.

- It is time we started working.
- It is high time he changed his behaviour.
