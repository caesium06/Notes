# English Grammar – Future Tense
## SSC CGL Preparation Notes

Future tense describes actions that will happen in the future, actions that will be in progress at a future time, actions that will be completed before a future point, and actions that will continue for a period up to a future point.

There are **four types of future tense**:

1. Simple Future Tense
2. Future Continuous Tense
3. Future Perfect Tense
4. Future Perfect Continuous Tense

---

# 1. Simple Future Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + will + V1 |
| Negative | Subject + will not + V1 |
| Interrogative | Will + Subject + V1? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Actions that will happen in the future.**

- I will visit Delhi tomorrow.
- She will attend the meeting.
- They will complete the project.

**Rule 2: Predictions about the future.**

- It will rain tomorrow.
- Technology will change our lives.
- He will become a successful engineer.

**Rule 3: Instant decisions and offers.**

- I will help you.
- I will answer the phone.
- Don't worry; I will carry your bag.

**Rule 4: Promises and willingness.**

- I will never forget you.
- I will support you.
- We will finish the work on time.

**Rule 5: First Conditional**

Structure:

If + Simple Present, Subject + will + V1.

- If it rains, I will stay home.
- If he studies hard, he will pass.
- If you work hard, you will succeed.

### Important SSC Rules

**1. Will + V1**

After will, always use the base form of the verb.

Incorrect: He will goes there.

Correct: He will go there.

Incorrect: She will completed the work.

Correct: She will complete the work.

**2. Shall and Will**

In modern English, will is commonly used with all subjects.

Traditionally:
- Shall: I / We
- Will: He / She / It / You / They

For SSC grammar questions, understand the traditional distinction, but remember that modern usage commonly accepts will with all persons.

**3. Shall for suggestions and offers**

- Shall we go for a walk?
- Shall I open the window?

**4. Will not = Won't**

- I will not attend the meeting.
- She won't come tomorrow.

# 2. Future Continuous Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + will be + V-ing |
| Negative | Subject + will not be + V-ing |
| Interrogative | Will + Subject + be + V-ing? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Actions that will be in progress at a particular future time.**

- I will be studying at 8 PM tomorrow.
- She will be travelling next week.
- They will be playing cricket this evening.

**Rule 2: Expected future activities.**

- He will be attending the conference tomorrow.
- My parents will be visiting us next month.

**Rule 3: Polite questions about someone's plans.**

- Will you be joining us for dinner?
- Will you be using the computer this evening?

### Examples

- At this time tomorrow, I will be writing my examination.
- This time next week, we will be travelling to Delhi.
- At 10 AM tomorrow, she will be attending an interview.

### Important Rule

Future Continuous structure:

Will + be + V-ing.

Incorrect: I will studying tomorrow.

Correct: I will be studying tomorrow.

Incorrect: She will be attend the meeting.

Correct: She will be attending the meeting.

---

# 3. Future Perfect Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + will have + V3 |
| Negative | Subject + will not have + V3 |
| Interrogative | Will + Subject + have + V3? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: An action that will be completed before a specific future time.**

- I will have completed my work by 6 PM.
- She will have reached home by tomorrow.
- They will have finished the project by next month.

**Rule 2: Completion before another future action.**

- By the time you arrive, I will have finished my homework.
- She will have left before we reach the station.

**Rule 3: Use of By and By the time**

Common expressions:

- By tomorrow
- By next week
- By 2030
- By the end of this month
- By the time he arrives

Examples:

- By next year, he will have completed his degree.
- By the time the train arrives, we will have reached the station.

### Important SSC Rule

Will have + V3.

Incorrect: He will have completed the work yesterday.

Correct: He will have completed the work by tomorrow.

---

# 4. Future Perfect Continuous Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + will have been + V-ing |
| Negative | Subject + will not have been + V-ing |
| Interrogative | Will + Subject + have been + V-ing? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: An action that will continue for a duration up to a specific future point.**

- By next month, I will have been working here for five years.
- By 2027, she will have been teaching for ten years.

**Rule 2: Emphasising the duration of a future activity.**

- By 8 PM, he will have been studying for three hours.
- By next December, they will have been living in Kochi for two years.

### Since and For

- Since = Starting point.
- For = Duration.

Examples:

- By June, I will have been working here since January.
- By June, I will have been working here for five months.

### Important SSC Rule

Will have been + V-ing.

Incorrect: He will have working for two hours.

Correct: He will have been working for two hours.

---

# 5. Comparison of All Four Future Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Future | Will + V1 | He will work tomorrow. |
| Future Continuous | Will be + V-ing | He will be working at 5 PM. |
| Future Perfect | Will have + V3 | He will have finished by 5 PM. |
| Future Perfect Continuous | Will have been + V-ing | He will have been working for two hours by 5 PM. |

### Understand the difference

Consider the verb "study":

1. I will study English. – Future action.
2. I will be studying English at 8 PM. – Action in progress at a future time.
3. I will have studied English by 8 PM. – Action completed before a future time.
4. I will have been studying English for two hours by 8 PM. – Duration of an action up to a future time.

# 6. Important SSC Exam Rules and Traps

### Rule 1: Do not use Will after If

In ordinary first conditional sentences, use Simple Present after if.

Incorrect: If he will come, I will meet him.

Correct: If he comes, I will meet him.

### Rule 2: Do not use Will after When

When referring to future time, use Simple Present in the time clause.

Incorrect: I will call you when I will reach home.

Correct: I will call you when I reach home.

### Rule 3: Other time conjunctions

Use Simple Present in future time clauses after:

- When
- Before
- After
- Until
- As soon as
- Once
- Unless

Examples:

- I will wait until he arrives.
- She will call me as soon as she reaches home.
- We will start after he comes.

### Rule 4: Unless

Unless means if not.

- Unless you work hard, you will fail.
- Unless he apologises, I will not forgive him.

Incorrect: Unless you will work hard, you will fail.

Correct: Unless you work hard, you will fail.

### Rule 5: No sooner / Hardly / Scarcely

These expressions are commonly used with past perfect and simple past, not as a general future tense construction.

Correct: No sooner had he arrived than the meeting started.

### Rule 6: By vs Until

**By** = Before or not later than a specified time.

- I will complete the work by Monday.

**Until** = Continuing up to a specified time.

- I will stay here until Monday.

### Rule 7: Be going to

Used for planned actions and predictions based on present evidence.

Structure: Subject + am/is/are + going to + V1.

Examples:

- I am going to buy a new laptop.
- Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.
- They are going to start a business.

### Rule 8: Present Continuous for Future Arrangements

Used for definite personal arrangements.

- I am meeting my manager tomorrow.
- She is travelling to Delhi next week.

### Rule 9: Simple Present for Timetables

- The train leaves at 6 AM tomorrow.
- The examination begins at 10 AM.

### Rule 10: Future in the Past

Use would + V1 when reporting a future action from a past viewpoint.

- He said that he would help me.
- She promised that she would return.

---

# 7. Will vs Going To vs Present Continuous

These are important distinctions for SSC sentence improvement.

| Construction | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Will + V1 | Instant decision / prediction / promise | I will help you. |
| Going to + V1 | Prior plan / evidence-based prediction | It is going to rain. |
| Present Continuous | Fixed arrangement | I am meeting him tomorrow. |
| Simple Present | Timetable / schedule | The train leaves tomorrow. |

### Four Structures to Memorise

1. Simple Future: Subject + will + V1
2. Future Continuous: Subject + will be + V-ing
3. Future Perfect: Subject + will have + V3
4. Future Perfect Continuous: Subject + will have been + V-ing

### SSC Preparation Strategy

Focus especially on:

- Will + V1
- Will be + V-ing
- Will have + V3
- Will have been + V-ing
- If / Unless clauses
- When / Until / As soon as clauses
- By vs Until
- Going to vs Will
- Present Continuous for future arrangements
- Future in the Past (Would + V1)

**Priority:** For SSC CGL, pay particular attention to the use of Simple Present after future time conjunctions and conditional conjunctions. These are common areas for error spotting and sentence improvement.
