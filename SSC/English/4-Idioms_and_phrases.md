# Idioms and Phrases – SSC CGL English

## 1. Introduction

An idiom is a group of words whose meaning is different from the literal meaning of the individual words.

A phrase is a group of words that conveys a particular meaning but may not form a complete sentence.

In SSC examinations, Idioms and Phrases are primarily tested through:

- Meaning of an idiom.
- Choosing the correct idiom for a given situation.
- Replacing an underlined phrase with an appropriate idiom.
- Identifying the correct usage of an idiom in a sentence.
- Understanding context-based expressions.

Important: Idioms should generally be understood as a complete expression rather than by interpreting individual words.

### Examples

| Idiom                    | Literal meaning                        | Actual meaning              |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| A piece of cake          | A piece of dessert                     | Something very easy         |

## 3. Important Confusing Idioms

SSC frequently tests idioms with similar meanings. Remember the distinctions.

| Idiom 1                | Meaning               | Idiom 2                    | Meaning                              |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Spill the beans        | Reveal a secret       | Let the cat out of the bag | Reveal a secret accidentally         |
| Once in a blue moon    | Very rarely           | At the drop of a hat       | Immediately                          |
| Beat around the bush   | Avoid the main point  | Come to the point          | Discuss the main issue               |
| See eye to eye         | Agree                 | Turn a deaf ear            | Ignore                               |
| Face the music         | Accept consequences   | Throw in the towel         | Admit defeat                         |
| Bury the hatchet       | End a quarrel         | Turn over a new leaf       | Change behaviour                     |
| Burn the midnight oil  | Work late             | Kill time                  | Waste or pass time                   |
| A dark horse           | Unexpected competitor | Black sheep                | Disgraceful member of a group        |
| A blessing in disguise | Hidden benefit        | A bolt from the blue       | Unexpected shocking event            |
| A close shave          | Narrow escape         | A narrow escape            | Escape from danger by a small margin |

### Particularly Important Differences

1\. Dark horse vs Black sheep

- Dark horse: A person whose abilities or intentions are unknown.
- Black sheep: A person who brings disgrace to a family or group.

2\. At the eleventh hour vs In the nick of time

- At the eleventh hour: At the last moment.
- In the nick of time: Just before it is too late.

3\. Burn the midnight oil vs Work against the clock

- Burn the midnight oil: Work late at night.
- Against the clock: Work quickly because of a deadline.

4\. A blessing in disguise vs A bolt from the blue

- Blessing in disguise: Something bad that eventually proves beneficial.
- Bolt from the blue: A sudden and unexpected event, usually shocking.

## 8. Common Mistakes in Idioms

1\. Don't interpret literally.\
"Spill the beans" does not mean dropping beans.

2\. Learn the complete expression.\
"At the drop of a hat" is the correct expression, not "at the drop of a cap".

3\. Understand the context.\
"A dark horse" refers to an unexpected or little-known competitor, not necessarily a dishonest person.

4\. Don't confuse similar expressions.\
"Turn a deaf ear" means deliberately ignore, while "all ears" means listen attentively.

5\. Remember fixed prepositions.

- In hot water
- At the eleventh hour
- On cloud nine
- Under the weather

6\. Understand positive and negative meanings.

- Apple of one's eye – Positive.
- Black sheep – Negative.
- Fair-weather friend – Negative.
- Gift of the gab – Positive.

## 12. Practice Test Scoring

Use the following SSC-style marking scheme:

- Correct answer: +1
- Incorrect answer: -0.25
- Skipped answer: 0

Formula:

\\[ \text{Score} = C - (W \times 0.25) \\]

Where:

- \\(C\\) = Correct answers
- \\(W\\) = Wrong answers

Maximum marks: 25

| Score    | Performance         |
| -------- | ------------------- |
| 22–25    | Excellent           |
| 18–21.75 | Very good           |
| 14–17.75 | Average             |
| 10–13.75 | Needs improvement   |
| Below 10 | Revise fundamentals |

## 13. Quick Revision Checklist

Before attempting an SSC CGL English mock test, ensure you can:

- Understand the difference between literal and idiomatic meanings.
- Recall common idioms related to human behaviour.
- Remember idioms related to success and failure.
- Identify idioms related to money and time.
- Understand idioms involving animals and colours.
- Differentiate between Dark Horse and Black Sheep.
- Differentiate between At the Eleventh Hour and In the Nick of Time.
- Recall idioms involving body parts.
- Understand idioms involving secrets and communication.
- Identify idioms in sentence-based questions.

Revision strategy: Learn 15–20 idioms daily, revise previously learned idioms, and practise mixed MCQs. Focus on remembering the meaning and using the idiom in a sentence rather than memorising words mechanically.
