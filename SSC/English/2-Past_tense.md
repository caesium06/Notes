# English Grammar – Past Tense
## SSC CGL Preparation Notes

Past tense describes actions that happened in the past, actions that were ongoing in the past, completed actions before another past action, and actions that continued for a period in the past.

There are **four types of past tense**:

1. Simple Past Tense
2. Past Continuous Tense
3. Past Perfect Tense
4. Past Perfect Continuous Tense

---

# 1. Simple Past Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + V2 + Object |
| Negative | Subject + did not + V1 |
| Interrogative | Did + Subject + V1? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Completed actions in the past.**

- He visited Delhi yesterday.
- I purchased a new car last month.
- She completed her homework.

**Rule 2: Actions that happened at a definite time in the past.**

- I met him in 2020.
- They arrived two hours ago.
- She left yesterday.

**Rule 3: Past habits and repeated actions.**

- He walked to school every day.
- My grandfather told us stories every night.

**Rule 4: A sequence of completed past actions.**

- He entered the room, sat down and opened his book.
- She woke up, prepared breakfast and left for work.

### Important SSC Rules

**1. Did + V1**

After did or did not, always use the first form of the verb.

Incorrect: He did not went to school.

Correct: He did not go to school.

Incorrect: Did she completed the work?

Correct: Did she complete the work?

**2. Past time indicators**

Common indicators of Simple Past:

- Yesterday
- Last night
- Last week
- Last year
- Two days ago
- In 2010
- Once
- Previously
- The other day

**3. Used to**

Used to describes past habits or situations that are no longer true.

- I used to play football.
- He used to live in Kochi.
- She used to wake up early.

Structure: Subject + used to + V1.

Incorrect: He used to played cricket.

Correct: He used to play cricket.

# 2. Past Continuous Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + was/were + V-ing |
| Negative | Subject + was/were + not + V-ing |
| Interrogative | Was/Were + Subject + V-ing? |

### Helping Verb Selection

| Subject | Helping verb |
|---|---|
| I | Was |
| He / She / It | Was |
| Singular noun | Was |
| We / You / They | Were |
| Plural noun | Were |

### Rules

**Rule 1: Actions that were happening at a particular time in the past.**

- I was studying at 8 PM yesterday.
- She was cooking when I called.
- They were playing football in the evening.

**Rule 2: An ongoing action interrupted by another action.**

Use Past Continuous for the longer action and Simple Past for the interrupting action.

- I was sleeping when the phone rang.
- She was reading when her friend arrived.
- They were playing when it started raining.

**Rule 3: Two actions happening simultaneously in the past.**

- While I was studying, my brother was watching television.
- She was cooking while he was cleaning the room.

**Rule 4: Background descriptions in the past.**

- The sun was shining and birds were singing.
- People were walking along the road.

### Important SSC Rule: While vs When

Generally:

- While + Past Continuous
- When + Simple Past (for an interrupting event)

Examples:

- While I was sleeping, the doorbell rang.
- I was sleeping when the doorbell rang.

Remember: This is a common pattern, not an absolute rule. When and while can also introduce other tense combinations depending on meaning.

---

# 3. Past Perfect Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + had + V3 |
| Negative | Subject + had not + V3 |
| Interrogative | Had + Subject + V3? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: An action completed before another action in the past.**

Use Past Perfect for the earlier action and Simple Past for the later action.

- The train had left before I reached the station.
- She had finished cooking before the guests arrived.
- The patient had died before the doctor arrived.

**Rule 2: An action completed before a specific time in the past.**

- By 2010, he had completed his degree.
- By the time I reached home, my father had left.

**Rule 3: Past Perfect with Before and After**

Examples:

- The thief escaped before the police arrived.
- The police arrived after the thief had escaped.

Important: Past Perfect is particularly useful to make the sequence of past events clear. It is not compulsory in every sentence containing before or after.

**Rule 4: No sooner...than**

Use:

No sooner + had + Subject + V3 + than + Simple Past.

- No sooner had he reached the station than the train departed.
- No sooner had she entered the room than everyone stood up.

**Rule 5: Hardly / Scarcely...when**

Use:

Hardly/Scarcely + had + Subject + V3 + when + Simple Past.

- Hardly had I reached home when it started raining.
- Scarcely had he arrived when the meeting began.

**Rule 6: It was the first time**

Use Past Perfect.

- It was the first time I had visited Delhi.
- It was the first time she had driven a car.

---

# 4. Past Perfect Continuous Tense

### Structure

| Sentence type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + had been + V-ing |
| Negative | Subject + had not been + V-ing |
| Interrogative | Had + Subject + been + V-ing? |

### Rules

**Rule 1: An action that started in the past and continued until another point in the past.**

- I had been studying for three hours when he arrived.
- She had been working there for five years before she resigned.

**Rule 2: Emphasising the duration of an earlier past action.**

- They had been waiting for two hours before the bus arrived.
- He had been practising for months before the competition.

**Rule 3: An earlier continuing action with a visible past result.**

- He was tired because he had been working all day.
- The ground was wet because it had been raining.

### Since and For

- Since = Starting point.
- For = Duration.

Examples:

- He had been working since morning.
- He had been working for five hours.

---

# 5. Comparison of All Four Past Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Past | Subject + V2 | He worked yesterday. |
| Past Continuous | Was/Were + V-ing | He was working. |
| Past Perfect | Had + V3 | He had worked. |
| Past Perfect Continuous | Had been + V-ing | He had been working for two hours. |

### Understand the difference

Consider the verb "study":

1. I studied English. – Completed past action.
2. I was studying English. – Action in progress at a past time.
3. I had studied English before the examination. – Completed before another past event.
4. I had been studying English for two hours before he arrived. – Duration of an action before another past event.

# 6. Important SSC Exam Rules and Traps

### Rule 1: Did + V1

Never use V2 after did or did not.

Incorrect: Did he went there?

Correct: Did he go there?

### Rule 2: Had + V3

After had, use the past participle (V3).

Incorrect: He had went home.

Correct: He had gone home.

### Rule 3: Both actions in the past

When two past actions occurred, use Past Perfect for the earlier action and Simple Past for the later action when their sequence needs to be expressed clearly.

- The train had left before we reached the station.
- The patient had died before the doctor arrived.

### Rule 4: No sooner...than

- No sooner had he arrived than it started raining.

Incorrect: No sooner he had arrived when it started raining.

### Rule 5: Hardly / Scarcely...when

- Hardly had I reached the station when the train arrived.
- Scarcely had she entered when the phone rang.

### Rule 6: Wish + Past Perfect

Use Past Perfect to express regret about a past situation.

- I wish I had studied harder.
- She wishes she had accepted the offer.

### Rule 7: If + Past Perfect

Used in third conditional sentences to describe an unreal past condition.

Structure:

If + Past Perfect, Subject + would have + V3.

- If I had studied, I would have passed.
- If he had worked harder, he would have succeeded.

Incorrect: If I would have studied, I would have passed.

### Rule 8: It is time / It is high time

Use Simple Past after these expressions.

- It is time we left.
- It is high time he changed his habits.

### Rule 9: Would rather + subject + Simple Past

Used to express preference about another person's action.

- I would rather you stayed here.
- She would rather he did not smoke.

### Rule 10: As if / As though

For an unreal situation in the past, use Past Perfect.

- He talks as if he had seen everything.
- She behaved as though she had known him for years.

# 10. Quick Revision Sheet

| Expression | Common tense association |
|---|---|
| Yesterday / Last week / Ago | Simple Past |
| At that time | Past Continuous (context dependent) |
| While | Past Continuous (common pattern) |
| When | Simple Past (interrupting event) |
| Before / After | Past Perfect (when sequence needs emphasis) |
| By the time | Past Perfect (earlier event) |
| For two hours before... | Past Perfect Continuous (duration) |
| No sooner...than | Past Perfect |
| Hardly...when | Past Perfect |
| Scarcely...when | Past Perfect |
| Used to | Simple Past construction |
| I wish I had... | Past Perfect |
| If I had... | Third conditional |

### Four Structures to Memorise

1. Simple Past: Subject + V2
2. Past Continuous: Subject + was/were + V-ing
3. Past Perfect: Subject + had + V3
4. Past Perfect Continuous: Subject + had been + V-ing

### SSC Preparation Strategy

Give special attention to:

- Did + V1
- Had + V3
- No sooner...than
- Hardly/Scarcely...when
- Since vs For
- Past Perfect vs Simple Past
- Irregular verb forms
- Third conditional sentences
- Used to + V1

**Priority:** Master the sequence of past actions and the correct forms of irregular verbs. These are especially useful for SSC CGL error spotting, sentence improvement and fill-in-the-blank questions.
